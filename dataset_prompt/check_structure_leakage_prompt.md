You are auditing a structured clinical case representation for diagnostic information leakage, as a second-pass check after an automated lexicon-based leak check already ran. Lexicon checks only catch exact diagnosis names/synonyms; you must catch what they cannot: paraphrased leaks, diagnosis-specific treatments that give away the answer, and cross-field/cross-subject consistency problems.

You will be given:
1. CONFIRMED DIAGNOSES for this patient - must not be revealed anywhere in the structured case, under any name, synonym, paraphrase, or implication.
2. The structured case (8 fields: demographics, chief_complaint, history_of_present_illness, past_medical_history, exposures_risk_factors, physical_exam_findings, lab_findings, imaging_findings).

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION - the goal of this structured case is a pre-diagnostic representation a clinician could read BEFORE knowing the diagnosis, to reason TOWARD it themselves. That means raw, literal, objective findings (exam signs, lab values, imaging descriptions, even highly specific or pathognomonic ones like "vegetations on the mitral valve" or "a fistulous tract between the coronary sinus and pulmonary artery") are REQUIRED evidence and are NEVER leaks on their own, no matter how strongly they point to a diagnosis - that predictive signal is the entire point of the dataset. Only flag "paraphrase" when the wording RENAMES the diagnosis category itself in different words (i.e. it functions as a label/conclusion, not a physical observation) - e.g. calling an arrhythmia "cardiac rhythm abnormalities", or calling cardiac arrest an "asystolic event". If a clinician would need to interpret raw evidence to reach the label, it is evidence, not a leak; if the phrase IS the label just reworded, it is a leak.

SECOND IMPORTANT DISTINCTION - some confirmed "diagnoses" are themselves just a symptom, sign, descriptive finding, structural/anatomical abnormality, or a complication/event-type diagnosis that is inherently self-descriptive - not an underlying disease/condition/organism (e.g. Ascites, Seizure or a specific seizure type/semiology, Xerostomia, Thrombocytopenia, Hyponatremia, Cardiac arrest, Headache, Cerebral edema, Iritis, Fistula, Endophthalmitis, Retinal detachment, Phthisis bulbi, Cataract, bowel obstruction, erectile dysfunction, Pneumothorax, Pleural effusion, Ventilator-associated pneumonia (it IS "new lung infection while ventilated"), Diffuse alveolar hemorrhage). For those, describing the finding/symptom/event itself - in ANY wording, including a paraphrase - is required, unavoidable content, NEVER a leak, because the "diagnosis" and the observable finding/event are the same thing. This exemption does NOT apply to genuine disease/condition/organism/mechanism diagnoses (e.g. Cirrhosis, Tuberculosis, Myelofibrosis, HIV infection, Multisystem inflammatory syndrome) - relabeling THOSE in different words is still a leak. When two or more confirmed diagnoses are similarly-described (e.g. two demyelinating conditions, two liver diseases), a vague paraphrase that doesn't clearly single out ONE of them over the others is NOT a leak either - it's genuinely ambiguous which one it would even reveal.

Check for these leak types:
- diagnosis_specific_treatment: a drug, dose, or procedure that, IN THE CONTEXT of this case's other findings, would let a clinician single out one of the confirmed diagnoses as the reason for treatment - even if that same drug is occasionally used elsewhere for unrelated conditions (e.g. IVIG 2 g/kg given for a febrile child with multisystem inflammatory findings is the recognizable Kawasaki/MIS-C regimen, even though IVIG is also used for unrelated things like ITP; a 4-drug anti-tuberculous regimen for TB; dapsone-based multidrug therapy for leprosy; antiretroviral therapy for HIV; clindamycin plus atovaquone/pyrimethamine for toxoplasmosis). Do NOT flag a generic, broadly-used treatment that gives no such distinguishing signal - e.g. plain "chemotherapy" or "received chemotherapy" with no drug/regimen named, generic "antibiotics" with no specific agent, "steroids"/corticosteroids alone with no other context, "supportive care", or "surgery" without further specifics. EXCEPTION: a generic drug-CATEGORY term (e.g. "antiparasitic therapy", "antifungal treatment", "antiviral medication") DOES count as a genuine leak if exactly ONE of this case's own confirmed diagnoses belongs to that category and the others do not - the category name then singles out that one diagnosis by elimination among this case's own list, even though the same category term would be too generic in isolation. It is NOT a leak if two or more of the confirmed diagnoses could equally be the reason for that category of treatment, or if none of them obviously are (e.g. it's plausibly for an unlisted secondary infection).
- paraphrase: wording that relabels a confirmed disease/condition diagnosis (not a symptom/finding-type one - see the distinction above) in different words rather than describing the raw finding that would be evidence for it (e.g. "cardiac rhythm abnormalities" for "Cardiac arrhythmia") - see the first distinction above too; do NOT use this category for a literal physical/imaging/lab finding, however diagnostic it is.
- combination_pattern: no single phrase leaks it, but the SPECIFIC diagnosis label or an unambiguous rewording of it is effectively spelled out by the combination - not simply that the combination happens to be typical evidence for the diagnosis (that is normal, required signal, not a leak).
- other: any other way a confirmed diagnosis is revealed or made obvious, other than through its supporting raw evidence.

Also separately check for a coherence problem unrelated to leaking:
- cross_field_inconsistency: a finding that doesn't belong to this patient/case (e.g. a multi-subject source document where a different patient's/animal's finding got merged in), or an internally contradictory or implausible detail.

Only flag things you're reasonably confident about - this is a precision-focused audit, not a hunt for anything loosely related to the diagnosis category. A field mentioning a common finding that happens to share a name with the diagnosis in a purely descriptive, non-conclusory way (e.g. "ascites" noted on exam when "Ascites" is itself one of the confirmed diagnoses) is NOT a leak worth flagging here - the lexicon checker already handles that pattern. When in doubt whether something is raw evidence vs. a relabeling, or whether a treatment is genuinely exclusive vs. broadly used, treat it as evidence/non-exclusive and do not flag it.
